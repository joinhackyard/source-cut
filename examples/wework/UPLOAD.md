In January 2019, SoftBank's investment valued WeWork at $47 billion. Seven months later, WeWork's own IPO filing showed another number: $47.2 billion in rent it had promised to pay, on leases of about fifteen years, while its members had signed up for about fifteen months. Six weeks after that, the IPO was withdrawn.

This is The Fall: the pitch, the filing, the fine print (votes, leases, the "We" trademark, a $500 million credit line), the six days in September 2019, SoftBank's rescue, and the long tail to bankruptcy in 2023. Public record only: every description of Adam Neumann comes from a named outlet or filing. With Neumann and Masayoshi Son in their own words.

Notes: $47 billion was a private post-money valuation. The rescue valuation of roughly $8 billion is CNBC's reporting, and the exit package of up to $1.7 billion is The Wall Street Journal's. Son's "my investment judgment was poor" is Reuters' translation. The $1.93 per dollar of revenue is our arithmetic from the S-1's 2018 expenses and revenue.
