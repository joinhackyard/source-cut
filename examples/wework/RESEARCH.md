Status labels:

- **VERIFIED**: primary document (SEC filing, company or SoftBank press release) or the person's own on-record words.
- **REPORTED**: a reputable outlet reports it, often from unnamed sources or its own calculation.
- **DISPUTED**: sources give different numbers, dates or wordings.
- **MYTH**: widely repeated, and the record does not support it.

**Rule for this episode:** every characterisation of Adam Neumann (his style, his conduct, why he left) must be attributed to a named outlet or document. The narrator states only what filings and releases say.

## 1. Fact sheet (38 facts)

### Peak: the $47 billion round (January 2019)

**F1. On 8 Jan 2019 WeWork said SoftBank's investment put its post-money valuation at $47 billion.** VERIFIED (company release).

- WeWork via Business Wire, "WeWork Closes $6 Billion Investment from SoftBank", 8 Jan 2019, https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20190108005751/en/ (read on a syndicated copy; see page file)
- "WeWork today announced it had received a $6 billion investment from SoftBank. $5 billion is in the form of primary growth capital and the remaining $1 billion in secondary capital which will be used to fund share purchases from investors and employees. WeWork's post-money valuation is now $47 billion."

**F2. Of that $6 billion, $4 billion had been announced earlier; the new money was about $2 billion.** VERIFIED.

- Same release: "This investment includes a previously announced $4 billion funding commitment from SoftBank in the form of a $1 billion convertible note and a $3 billion warrant. WeWork and its subsidiaries have now raised more than $10 billion in total commitments from SoftBank since 2017."
- CNBC, Sara Salinas, 8 Jan 2019, https://www.cnbc.com/2019/01/08/wework-rebranding-as-the-we-company-softbank-invests-2-billion.html: "SoftBank plans to invest $2 billion more in WeWork ... Most of SoftBank's recent investment is at a post-money valuation of $47 billion, but $1 billion of the investment was at a lower, pre-money valuation of $20 billion."

**F3. A much bigger SoftBank deal had fallen through weeks earlier.** REPORTED.

- Fast Company, Katrina Brooker, 8 Jan 2019, https://www.fastcompany.com/90289512/exclusive-wework-to-rebrand-to-the-we-company-in-wake-of-disappointing-funding-news/
- "The numbers on the table ranged as high as $20 billion, at a valuation that hovered close to $50 billion." / "Son told him that the deal SoftBank and WeWork had spent months negotiating was no longer viable."

**F4. Son, in the January release, credited "Adam's vision".** VERIFIED (SoftBank CEO's words in the release).

- Business Wire, 8 Jan 2019: "WeWork has already experienced unparalleled growth and we are confident that with Adam's vision and this growth capital the company will be able to aggressively pursue the enormous market opportunity ahead of them," said Masayoshi Son, Chairman & CEO of SoftBank Group Corp.

**F5. The same day WeWork became The We Company, with the mission "to elevate the world's consciousness".** VERIFIED.

- WeWork Newsroom, "The beginning of a new story", 8 Jan 2019, https://www.wework.com/newsroom/wecompany (read via Wayback)
- "The We Company's guiding mission will be to elevate the world's consciousness. Living a conscious life means choosing to live proactively and with purpose."

**F6. Neumann, in his own words, January 2019: a downturn is "an opportunity".** VERIFIED (his words, as quoted by Fast Company).

- Fast Company, 8 Jan 2019: "When 2019 comes and if the world goes into a real downturn, the one thing you are not going to see us do is be afraid or slow down or take less risks," he says. ... "For me, a downturn is not scary. It's an opportunity," Neumann says.

**F7. Fast Company's figures for the first nine months of 2018: revenue of $1.25 billion, losses of $1.22 billion.** REPORTED.

- Fast Company, 8 Jan 2019: "For the first three quarters of 2018, it generated $1.25 billion in revenues but lost $1.22 billion."

**F8. The We Company filed its S-1 with the SEC on 14 Aug 2019.** VERIFIED.

**F11. 2018: revenue $1.82 billion, net loss $1.93 billion.** VERIFIED.

**F12. 2018 total expenses were $3.51 billion against $1.82 billion of revenue; the operating loss was $1.69 billion.** VERIFIED (S-1 table).

**F14. Lease obligations: $47.2 billion, on leases averaging about 15 years.** VERIFIED.

- S-1 risk factors: "The average length of the initial term of our U.S. leases is approximately 15 years, and our future undiscounted minimum lease cost payment obligations under signed operating and finance leases was $47.2 billion as of June 30, 2019."

**F15. Against that, member commitments averaged "more than 15 months", and the committed revenue backlog was $4.0 billion.** VERIFIED.

- S-1: "the average commitment term of our membership agreements has nearly doubled from approximately eight months as of December 1, 2017 to more than 15 months as of June 1, 2019." / "We had committed revenue backlog of $4.0 billion as of June 30, 2019".

**F16. The trademark payment: WE Holdings LLC (Neumann's entity) assigned the "we" trademarks to the company for partnership interests worth about $5.9 million, "in lieu of paying cash".** VERIFIED.

- S-1 (14 Aug 2019), related-party section: "In July 2019, WE Holdings LLC assigned residual rights related to "we" family trademarks to the Company ... In consideration of this contribution and in lieu of paying cash, the Company issued to WE Holdings LLC partnership interests in the We Company Partnership with a fair market value of approximately $5.9 million, which was determined pursuant to a third-party appraisal."

**F17. Amendment No. 1 said the trademark deal had been unwound "at Adam's direction".** VERIFIED.

**F18. Related-party leases: four buildings leased from landlord entities in which Neumann had an ownership interest.** VERIFIED.

**F19. Voting control: high-vote shares carried 20 votes each, and Neumann would control a majority of the vote.** VERIFIED.

- S-1: "The Class A common stock has one vote per share and the Class B common stock and Class C common stock (together, "high-vote stock") have twenty votes per share."

**F20. Succession: if Neumann died or was permanently disabled, a committee including Rebekah Neumann would pick the next CEO.** VERIFIED.

### Disagreements and the safest version to narrate

- **"$47 billion".** It is the **post-money private valuation** of SoftBank's January 2019 investment (F1), not a market price. Part of the same deal was priced off a $20 billion pre-money figure (CNBC, F2). **Safest:** "SoftBank's investment valued WeWork at $47 billion."
- **Rescue valuation.** CNBC: "$7.5 billion and $8 billion on a pre-funding basis"; Fortune/Bloomberg: SoftBank valued it at $7.8 billion at end-September; Bloomberg TV: "less than 8 billion dollars". The release gives no valuation. **Safest:** "roughly $8 billion, according to CNBC."
- **Rescue size.** The release lists $5B + up to $3B + $1.5B. Fortune called it "a $9.5 billion rescue package"; CNBC (2021) said "$10 billion for an 80% stake". **Safest:** list the three parts, or "about $9.5 billion" with Fortune named.
- **Son's words, 6 Nov 2019.** Two translations of the same press conference: Reuters "My investment judgment was poor in many ways and I am reflecting deeply on that"; Bloomberg "There was a problem with my own judgment, that's something I have to reflect on". Son spoke in Japanese; these are interpreters'/reporters' renderings. **Safest:** quote Reuters and credit "Reuters translation, via CNBC".
- **Rescue date.** WeWork's release is timestamped 22 Oct 2019 (US), SoftBank's 23 Oct 2019 (Japan). **Safest:** "in late October 2019" or "on October 22".
- **The trademark money.** It was **partnership interests worth about $5.9 million, "in lieu of paying cash"** (S-1), unwound by Amendment No. 1 (Sept 2019). CNBC (2021) loosely says "planned to charge WeWork $6 million". **Safest:** "a $5.9 million payment in company equity for the 'We' trademark, which he returned weeks later."
- **Votes per share.** 20 (S-1, Aug) → 10 (S-1/A2, 13 Sep). CNBC on 24 Sep reported a further cut "from 10:1 to 3:1" (source to CNBC; not in a filing I read). **Safest:** "20, then 10".
- **"Pulled the IPO".** Roadshow postponed mid-September (reported); withdrawal announced and Form RW filed 30 Sep 2019. **Safest:** "On September 30, 2019, WeWork withdrew its IPO filing."
- **DealBook "2022".** The interview was **9 Nov 2021** (DealBook Online Summit). The **Emmy** for it was announced Sept 2022 (CNBC clip `lTo2-folmcc`, 30 Sep 2022). Do not caption it "2022".

## 5. Common errors and the safest wording

1. **"WeWork was worth $47 billion."** It was a private **post-money valuation** set by SoftBank's January 2019 investment. Say "SoftBank's investment valued it at $47 billion."
2. **"The $47 billion valuation and the $47 billion in leases are the same number."** Coincidence. Leases were **$47.2 billion** of future undiscounted payments (S-1). Say "It also owed $47.2 billion in future rent."
3. **"Neumann charged WeWork $5.9 million in cash for the 'We' trademark."** It was **partnership interests**, "in lieu of paying cash", and it was **unwound** "at Adam's direction" (S-1/A1). Say "$5.9 million in company equity, later returned."
4. **"WeWork's IPO failed / flopped on the market."** It never priced or traded. It was **withdrawn** (Form RW, 30 Sep 2019).
5. **"Neumann was fired."** The company said he "decided to step back"; CNBC reported Son "led the charge to remove" him. Say "He stepped down under pressure from investors, CNBC reported."
6. **"Neumann walked away with $1.7 billion."** That was a **reported** package "as much as $1.7 billion" (WSJ via CNBC). SoftBank later dropped the $3B tender (April 2020, CNBC). Attribute it, and say "up to".
7. **"SoftBank bought WeWork for $8 billion."** No. SoftBank committed new money for about 80% ownership; "about $8 billion" is the **reported** implied valuation.
8. **"Son said 'my judgment was poor'."** Close: Reuters' translation is "My investment judgment was poor in many ways". Bloomberg's is different. Credit the translation.
9. **"WeWork IPO'd in 2021."** It merged with a SPAC (BowX) and listed on the NYSE on 21 Oct 2021. Not an IPO.
10. **"WeWork went bankrupt worldwide."** The filing covered the US and Canada; "locations outside of the U.S. and Canada are not part of this process" (WeWork release).
11. **"WeWork shut down."** It emerged from Chapter 11 on 11 June 2024 and still operates; old shareholders were wiped out.
12. **"DealBook 2022 interview."** It was **9 Nov 2021**; the Emmy came in 2022.
13. **"The S-1 mentions Adam 169 times."** That is a count of a text file (mine gives 169 for "Adam"); counts vary by method. Avoid or show the method.
14. **"Neumann lost control when he resigned."** On 24 Sep he stayed non-executive chairman with high-vote shares; the board took voting control of his shares under the **October** SoftBank deal.

## 6. Suggested outline (about 8 minutes), verified facts only

**Cold open (0:00–0:35).** Card 1, highlight "post-money valuation is now $47 billion". Then Card 7, "$47.2 billion". VO: "In January 2019, SoftBank's money valued WeWork at $47 billion. Seven months later, its own filing showed a second number: $47.2 billion in rent it had promised to pay." Title.

**Chapter 1 — The pitch (0:35–1:50).** CBS 2016 (A3): "this is for the we generation ... and the money tends to follow"; "this is not a trend this is the future of work". TechCrunch 2017 (A1): "we used to say that we're a community company". January 2019: $6B close, $47B post-money (Card 1); The We Company and "elevate the world's consciousness" (Card 2). Neumann on CNBC (A2): decisions "three months forward". Fast Company: "a downturn is not scary. It's an opportunity" (Card 3).

**Chapter 2 — The filing (1:50–3:30).** S-1 filed 14 Aug 2019 (Card 4). Mission line (Card 5). Revenue $1.82B, net loss $1.93B; $1.93 spent per $1 of revenue (F12; Card 6). The mismatch: 15-year leases, $47.2B, against 15-month member commitments and a $4.0B backlog (Card 7). Son on CNBC in March 2019 (A6): losing money because "they're investing" — "it's like a subscription".

**Chapter 3 — The fine print (3:30–4:50).** Twenty votes per share; Neumann controls a majority (Card 11). Four buildings leased from entities he part-owned (Card 10). The "we" trademark for $5.9M of equity (Card 8), then unwound "at Adam's direction" (Card 9). Banks underwriting the IPO had lent him up to $500M (F21). 13 Sep: votes cut from 20 to 10 "in response to market feedback" (F23; A9).

**Chapter 4 — The fall (4:50–6:20).** 24 Sep: he steps down; "the scrutiny directed toward me has become a significant distraction" (Card 12). CBS: about five months of cash (F27, attributed). 30 Sep: Form RW, "no longer wishes to conduct a public offering" (Card 13; A8). Late Oct: SoftBank's $5B + $3B + $1.5B, ~80% ownership, Neumann a board observer (Card 14); CNBC: roughly $8B (F31). 6 Nov: Son, "My investment judgment was poor in many ways" (Card 15; A7 for picture).

**Chapter 5 — The long tail (6:20–7:20).** SPAC with BowX, ~$9B, NYSE "WE" on 21 Oct 2021 (F34). Neumann at DealBook, Nov 2021: "multiple lessons and multiple regrets" (A4/A5). Aug 2023: "substantial doubt" (Card 16). 6 Nov 2023: Chapter 11 in New Jersey (Card 17; A10). 11 Jun 2024: emerges; old equity "cancelled" (Card 18).

**The lesson (7:20–8:00).** Unit economics always catch up.

1. **Check what a dollar of revenue costs you.** In 2018 WeWork spent $3.51B to earn $1.82B (S-1). Neumann in 2017 said that with a margin "you can actually choose when to be profitable" (A1); the filing showed it had not chosen to yet.
2. **Match how long you owe with how long you're owed.** Fifteen-year leases, $47.2B, against members committed for about 15 months (S-1). CNBC's Bosa in 2023: "holding on to long-term leases and turning them around for flexible, short-term rentals was always a risky proposition" (A10).
3. **A valuation is a price someone paid, not proof the model works.** $47B in January 2019; roughly $8B in October (CNBC); equity cancelled in 2024 (8-K). Son: "My investment judgment was poor" (Reuters).

### 50-second Short spine

- 0–5 s: Card 1, highlight "$47 billion". Text: "Worth $47 billion. Then it filed its paperwork."
- 5–13 s: Card 6. VO: "The IPO filing showed a $1.9 billion loss in 2018, on $1.8 billion of revenue."
- 13–22 s: Card 7. VO: "It owed $47.2 billion in rent on 15-year leases. Its members signed for about 15 months."
- 22–30 s: Card 8 → Card 9. VO: "The founder's company got $5.9 million in equity for the 'We' trademark. He gave it back."
- 30–38 s: Card 12 → Card 13. VO: "He stepped down. Six days later the IPO was withdrawn."
- 38–45 s: Card 15 (A7 picture). VO: "SoftBank's rescue valued it at about $8 billion. Its CEO: 'My investment judgment was poor.'"
